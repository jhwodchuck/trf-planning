# Per-pass SVGs

Ten pass-section SVGs, three detached amenity-section SVGs, and two accepted walkway SVGs, generated from shared definitions so each can be refined and freely repositioned on a separate master canvas.

The independently movable amenity pieces are:

- A.'s 12 × 25 ft, 300 sq ft Yoga / smoking-tent section;
- S. and T.'s 10 × 10 ft, 100 sq ft portapotty end; and
- S. and S.'s 10.2 × 10 ft, 102 sq ft fire-pit end.

The accepted arrangement reconnects those pieces as follows:

- A.'s Yoga section shares a 25 ft boundary with her sleeping section, so no extra connector area is required.
- S. and S.'s shower and fire-pit sections are joined by a routed **66 sq ft** connector, approximately 34.29 ft long and 1.92 ft wide.
- S. and T.'s 20 ft circular yurt and portapotty sections are joined by a routed **100 sq ft** connector, approximately 67.65 ft long and 1.48 ft wide.

The two routed polygons are allocation connectors, not verified pedestrian or accessible walkways. S. and T. retains **85.8407 sq ft unplaced** for yurt entry, platform edge, anchors, ropes, or other setup clearance. The visible pieces total **5,914.1593 sq ft**; adding that reserve preserves the full **6,000 sq ft** allocation.

## Files

- `<id>.svg` — one self-contained public drawing per pass section, detached piece, or connector. Each file uses its own local coordinate system with a small margin around its boundary.
- `passes-manifest.json` — machine-readable index of the ten pass sections, three detached pieces, and two connectors, including household color, footprint size, viewBox size, allocation group, reserve, and default position.

## Regenerating

These files are generated by [`scripts/build_pass_svgs.py`](../../../scripts/build_pass_svgs.py), which does not touch the network — it only re-emits the geometry already defined in that script:

```bash
python scripts/build_pass_svgs.py
```

To change source geometry, edit `PASSES`. To change the freeform sections or accepted routes, edit `LAYOUT_PASSES`, `DETACHED_PARTS`, and `CONNECTOR_PARTS`. Do not hand-edit generated SVGs or the manifest.

After accepting a new `saved-camp-v0.1.json` arrangement, regenerate the
georeferenced group viewer and KML from that same layout:

```bash
python scripts/build_group_site_from_saved_layout.py
python scripts/build_group_site_kml.py
```

This keeps `viewer/index.html` and `viewer/pass-layout.html` on the same pass
positions and rotations while preserving the road, setback, and west-limit
reference layers.

## Interactive master map

Open [`maps/viewer/pass-layout.html`](../../viewer/pass-layout.html) (served over http, not `file://`) to drag, rotate, and rearrange the ten pass sections, three detached pieces, and two connectors on one local-foot canvas. Save/load and share links preserve all fifteen positions. Legacy ten- and thirteen-piece share links remain accepted; missing connector positions fall back to the saved default.

This tool remains **planning-grade, not survey-grade**. Positions stay in local feet, while the aerial and supplied road coordinates share the same affine WGS84 alignment. Its displayed road is the 25-point user-supplied coordinate trace; the 10 ft and 14 ft lines are approximate perpendicular offsets. Use `maps/viewer/index.html` (the "Group site v0.1" layer) and `maps/data/group-site-v0.1.geojson` for the geographic-coordinate version.

The cyan hard-west-limit line comes from the two user-supplied WGS84 coordinates recorded in `planning/site-control-road-setback.md`. The viewer extrapolates that alignment across the canvas and clamps each pass's rotated view box to its east side.

`saved-camp-v0.1.json` is the accepted starting arrangement used on first load and by **Reset to accepted camp default**. Its ten `passes`, three `detached_parts`, and two `connectors` entries also drive the georeferenced GeoJSON and KML builders, keeping the freeform and geographic views on the same accepted baseline.
