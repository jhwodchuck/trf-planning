## Physical footprint

One generator's rectangular base envelope is approximately:

- `24.5 × 18.25 = 447.1 sq in`
- `447.1 / 144 = 3.11 sq ft`

Two generator bodies therefore occupy roughly **6.2 sq ft** before any spacing, cooling airflow, exhaust path, fueling access, cords, fuel containers, fire protection, or safe separation from occupied structures.

The body footprint is not an acceptable proxy for the required operating area.

## Electrical baseline

Two P4500i units may be operated using compatible manufacturer-approved parallel equipment, subject to the generator and camper connection configuration. Before relying on this arrangement, document:

- exact parallel kit model;
- outlet used and output rating;
- camper inlet and adapter configuration;
- neutral/ground behavior;
- overcurrent protection;
- cable gauge, length, and trip protection;
- measured running and startup current for each A/C;
- whether the hard-start devices are installed and functioning;
- operating behavior with both A/C units, converter/charger, refrigerator, microwave, and other loads.

A successful load test does not establish safe generator placement.

## Carbon-monoxide and fire constraints

The layout must follow the current Cummins manual and applicable TRF requirements. Government guidance from CDC/CPSC generally advises operating portable generators outdoors, at least **20 ft from doors, windows, and vents**, with exhaust directed away from buildings and occupied spaces.

The Cummins manual/specification must also be checked for combustible-material and airflow clearances; current manual guidance has been reported as requiring at least **5 ft from combustible material** during operation.

Use the stricter applicable requirement. Verify the manual revision for the actual generators.

## Screening rule

The requested fence is for visual screening, not acoustic or weather enclosure. It must not:

- surround the generators tightly;
- trap or redirect exhaust toward the camper, canopy, road, neighbor, or occupied area;
- block cooling-air intake or discharge;
- create a combustible surface inside a required clearance;
- restrict fueling, shutdown, inspection, or firefighting access;
- hide the generator from routine safety checks;
- create a trip hazard with cords or feet/posts.

A staggered open screen, placed outside all required clearances and with exhaust pointed into unobstructed open air, is more plausible than a wraparound fence. TRF approval is still required.

## Major feasibility concern

A nominal 600-sq-ft plot could be, for example, 20 × 30 ft. A 31-ft-7-in camper body already exceeds one dimension of that example, and a 20-ft separation from openings may be impossible if the generators must remain inside the same compact boundary.

Before drawing the generators near the camper's nose, determine whether:

1. TRF provides a designated or shared generator location;
2. equipment may occupy an approved extension or different configuration;
3. a safer power method is required;
4. the plot can be shaped or located to provide adequate separation;
5. nearby doors/windows/vents can be avoided without putting exhaust toward neighbors.

Do not reduce a safety clearance merely to make the drawing fit.
