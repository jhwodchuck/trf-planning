## What the imagery adds

The dated view materially improves planning context compared with the 2022
NAIP backdrop:

- it shows the campground occupied during the 2025 season;
- multiple light-colored camper, trailer, tent, or canopy roofs are visible
  among the trees;
- established dirt roads curve and branch through the Havok area;
- usable clearings are irregular and constrained by mature tree canopy; and
- prior disturbed or occupied pads are better candidates for 2026 equipment
  than an unconstrained rectangular grid.

The image does **not** establish which visible structure belonged to which
household, the approved 2025 pass boundaries, tree-trunk coordinates, road-edge
coordinates, or the exact 2026 usable area.

## Consequences for the 2026 plan

1. Keep the ten full 600 sq ft pass entitlements, but treat every placement
   except J. and C.'s anchored polygon as a candidate.
2. Prefer previously occupied/disturbed clearings before proposing new
   equipment positions under dense canopy.
3. Preserve the visible road network and a continuous circulation/emergency
   access path; do not rely on the current 21.7 ft straight road sample to
   represent the full curved road edge.
4. Field-mark tree trunks, roots, low branches, ruts, drainage paths, and the
   actual road edge before converting imagery observations into boundaries.
5. Identify which 2025 roofs/footprints belonged to this group before using
   them as household-specific placement evidence.
6. Import `maps/overlays/group-site-v0.1.kml` into Google Earth as a **local**
   KML layer to compare the 2026 candidate polygons with the dated view.

## Image-handling rule

The repository records the Google Earth link and observations but does not
download or rehost Google's aerial tiles. The KML contains only project-owned
planning geometry. Keep it local when comparing the plan in Google Earth and
retain Google's on-screen attribution in any permitted screenshot or print.
